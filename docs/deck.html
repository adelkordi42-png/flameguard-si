FlameGuard SI

A fire-safety decision assistant for freefall

NASA has decades of microgravity combustion data. It tells you what happened in a test. It does not tell you what to do about the cabin in front of you.

The engine is a Jev-compatible typed decision contract: four noul thresholds, one choice, one score. It is selectable (TypeSafe Jev, OpenJev, or published-threshold margins). Calibration is validated against NASA-derived outcomes; it is not inherited from the engine.

Cache-first: every response is keyed by a hash of state + question contract. A miss with no network returns needs_human_review, never a fabricated answer. The endpoint is POST /v1/systemone; the brief’s /v1/jev:run returns 404.

Calibration is fitted on the 70% combiner-fit split and measured on the held-out 30%. It is not inherited from the engine. The suppression strategy is a deterministic policy, not a learned classifier. This is not a flight-qualified procedure.

Roadmap

What comes after the demo

  • More Tier A labels: replace derived FLEX/Saffire labels with explicit published outcomes wherever the papers expose them.
  • SoFIE/PSI ingestion: add the remaining experiment families and a stable schema adapter.
  • Active learning: feed human review decisions back as labels; monitor drift by family.
  • Onboard / edge: quantize the combiner and serve the same cached contract with a local fallback.
  • Human-in-the-loop: make the abstention queue and reason codes the primary workflow, not an exception.
